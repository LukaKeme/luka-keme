How it can feel

Anxiety can show up in the body: a tight chest, a racing heart, a stomach that won't settle, trouble falling asleep. It can also live in your thoughts, with the same worry going round and round, or a sense that something bad is about to happen.

Some anxiety is part of being human. It becomes a problem when it starts to decide for you: what you avoid, how you sleep, how much room is left for anything else.

In psychological consultations we look at what your anxiety is about and what it is trying to protect. Once you understand it, it often feels less frightening, and you have more freedom to choose how to respond.

What people with anxiety often find hardest

  • Not being able to switch off

    Worrying can feel useful, as if stopping would mean being careless. We can look at what the worry does for you, and what it costs you.

  • Avoiding things

    Avoidance brings relief now and makes life smaller later. Together we can notice what you have started to avoid, and take small steps back at your own pace.

  • Anxiety in the body

    Physical symptoms can be frightening in themselves, and the fear of them adds more anxiety. Understanding how the nervous system reacts to stress often helps.

  • Being hard on yourself

    Many anxious people are very self-critical, and ashamed of being anxious at all. We can notice that inner voice and find a kinder way to talk to yourself.

When something else may help more

I may not be the right person if you are looking for crisis intervention, a formal diagnosis, medication or changes to your medication treatment, or very structured short-term consultations. In those cases, I could only refer you to a more suitable specialist.

If you have panic attacks very often, or anxiety stops you leaving home, a structured cognitive behavioural therapy programme may be the best first step. I can help you find one.
