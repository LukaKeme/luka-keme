How it can feel

Some people feel everything at full volume. Joy, anger, sadness or shame arrive fast and strong, and can change within hours, sometimes within minutes.

It can be tiring, for you and for the people close to you. Afterwards you might feel embarrassed, or wonder which feeling was the real you.

Strong emotions are not a flaw. They often come together with sensitivity, empathy and a lot of energy. In consultations we look at what sets them off, what they tell you, and how to stay with them without being swept away.

Alongside consultations I research emotion regulation in bipolar disorder with the ENBER network, so this topic is close to my heart.

What people with intense emotions often find hardest

  • Feelings that come too fast

    When a feeling hits before you can think, it is hard to choose what to do with it. We can slow that moment down and notice what happens just before it.

  • Regret afterwards

    Saying or doing something in the heat of the moment, then feeling ashamed. We can look at this with curiosity rather than blame.

  • Relationships

    Strong feelings can make closeness both precious and difficult. We can explore what you need from others and how to ask for it.

  • Wondering what is normal

    Many people wonder whether their mood swings are part of their personality or something more. We can talk about it openly, and if an assessment might help, I can tell you where to go.

When something else may help more

I may not be the right person if you are looking for crisis intervention, a formal diagnosis, medication or changes to your medication treatment, or very structured short-term consultations. In those cases, I could only refer you to a more suitable specialist.

If your mood changes come with weeks of very high energy and little sleep, or with thoughts of harming yourself, please see a psychiatrist first.
