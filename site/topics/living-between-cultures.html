How it can feel

Moving abroad can be an amazing experience. Many people dream of working remotely and living as a digital nomad.

It is also completely natural for such a move to raise questions inside. How important is my home country to me? How do I live far away from parents who are getting older? And every day you may be trying to express yourself in a foreign language.

Sometimes even a move you dreamed about for a long time can feel lonely. Finding your people takes time, and so does getting used to a new home.

In psychological consultations we can explore these feelings, and the inner change that such a big outer change inevitably brings.

What people who move abroad often find hardest

Research on people living and working abroad points to some common difficulties.

  • Loneliness and finding your people

    A new place often means building a support network from scratch. In consultations we can look at what makes connection hard right now, and what you need from the people around you.

  • Expressing yourself in another language

    Many people feel less like themselves when they talk about feelings in a second language. You can choose Lithuanian or English, and switch whenever you like.

  • Belonging and identity

    You may wonder where home is now, and which parts of you have changed. We can explore how you see yourself, without rushing to an answer.

  • Everyday stress in a new culture

    Different rules at work, a different pace of life and small daily misunderstandings add up. Together we can notice how this affects you and what helps.
